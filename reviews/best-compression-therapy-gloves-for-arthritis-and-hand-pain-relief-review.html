Comprehensive Buying Guide

Understanding Compression Therapy

Compression therapy works by applying gentle, consistent pressure to affected areas, which helps reduce swelling, improve circulation, and provide support to joints and muscles. For arthritis sufferers, this can translate to reduced pain, improved mobility, and better quality of life.

Key Factors to Consider

Compression Level

Compression is typically measured in mmHg (millimeters of mercury). For arthritis gloves, you'll generally find:

  • Light compression (8-15 mmHg): Suitable for mild symptoms and general comfort
  • Moderate compression (15-20 mmHg): Ideal for most arthritis sufferers
  • Firm compression (20-30 mmHg): For severe symptoms, usually requiring medical consultation

Material and Breathability

Look for materials that balance compression with comfort:

  • Cotton blends: Soft and breathable but may lose elasticity
  • Spandex/Lycra: Excellent stretch and recovery
  • Nylon: Durable and moisture-wicking
  • Bamboo fiber: Natural antimicrobial properties

Design Features

Consider these important design elements:

  • Open vs. closed fingertips: Open tips allow better dexterity
  • Seam placement: Seamless or flat seams prevent irritation
  • Wrist length: Longer gloves provide additional support
  • Thumb coverage: Important for comprehensive hand support

Sizing and Fit

Proper sizing is crucial for effectiveness:

  1. Measure your hand circumference at the widest part (usually across the knuckles)
  2. Measure wrist circumference
  3. Check manufacturer size charts as they can vary significantly
  4. Consider that compression gloves should feel snug but not painful
  5. Remember that some stretch and conform to your hand over time

When to Wear Compression Gloves

Daily Activities: Many users benefit from wearing gloves during routine tasks like typing, cooking, or gardening.

Sleep: Overnight wear can help reduce morning stiffness, though some find it takes getting used to.

Exercise: Compression can provide support during physical activities and sports.

Weather Changes: Many arthritis sufferers experience increased pain during weather changes, making gloves particularly helpful during these times.

Care and Maintenance

To maximize the lifespan of your compression gloves:

  • Hand wash in cool water when possible
  • Use mild detergent without fabric softeners
  • Air dry away from direct heat or sunlight
  • Replace every 3-6 months or when compression decreases
  • Consider having multiple pairs for rotation

Medical Considerations

While compression gloves are generally safe, consult with a healthcare provider if you have:

  • Severe circulatory problems
  • Open wounds or infections on your hands
  • Severe arthritis requiring medical management
  • Numbness or tingling that worsens with compression
  • Any concerns about whether compression therapy is appropriate for your condition
